# Dial-a-ride state of the art, 2025–2026: what changed and what it means for MobiRoute

**Compiled:** 2026-09-10 · **Scope:** exact-method formulations, transfers and synchronization, learning-based heuristics, approximation results, deployed paratransit systems.

This review complements `paratransit-research-2024-2026.md`. Each card states
what the work does and, separately, what MobiRoute may and may not claim from
it. Nothing here is a MobiRoute result.

---

## 1. Tighter exact formulations

### Location-augmented event-based MILP
Gaul, D., Klamroth, K., Pfeiffer, C., Stiglmayr, M., Schulz, A. — *A tight
formulation for the dial-a-ride problem*. **European Journal of Operational
Research 321(2): 363–382 (2025)**, DOI `10.1016/j.ejor.2024.09.028`; preprint
arXiv:2308.11285.

The event-based graph is augmented with location information (LAEB / ALAEB),
which tightens the LP relaxation and reduces the number of nodes needed per
request state. Reported as competitive with, and on several instance families
superior to, earlier three-index and event-based models.

**Relevance.** This is the reference point for any exact benchmark profile we
build (profile A). If we ever want a defensible optimality gap on Cordeau-style
instances, this is the formulation family to reproduce — not our insertion
kernel.

### Synchronized visits
Zhao, Y., Puchinger, J., Leus, R. — *The Dial-a-Ride Problem with Synchronized
Visits*. arXiv:2601.00498 (2026).

Four models compared — arc-based, event-based, a time–space–event formulation
and a fragment-based formulation — plus dynamic discretization discovery for the
time-indexed variants. Synchronization means a request may require two vehicles
or a staff resource present simultaneously.

**Relevance.** Directly maps onto attendant/escort requirements in social taxi
(a passenger who must be met by staff). MobiRoute does not model synchronized
visits today; this is the correct citation when the roadmap item is raised.

### Electric autonomous DARP discretization
*New Formulations and Discretization Insights for the Electric Autonomous
Dial-a-Ride Problem*. arXiv:2601.03282 (2026).

Studies how time and charge discretization granularity changes both tractability
and solution quality for E-ADARP.

**Relevance.** Our travel matrix is minute-discretized; the paper is the honest
citation for why discretization is a modelling decision with a measurable cost,
not an implementation detail.

### Limited pickups per trip
Zhao, Y., Wang, S., Wei, L., Leus, R. — *The dial-a-ride problem with limited
pickups per trip*. **EJOR 327(3): 776–790 (2025)**, DOI
`10.1016/j.ejor.2025.05.051`.

**Relevance.** Formalizes the operator rule "no more than k pickups on one
run", which is exactly the kind of constraint our operator policy contract
carries. Cite when explaining why profile B is not comparable to literature
results.

## 2. Transfers and line-based service

Gaul, D. et al. — *The Line-Based Dial-a-Ride Problem with Transfers*, OASIcs
**ATMOS 2025**, DOI `10.4230/OASIcs.ATMOS.2025.17`; branch-and-price treatment
in arXiv:2603.06295 (2026).

Requests may be split across vehicles running fixed lines, with transfer nodes.

**Relevance.** Transfers are the standard answer to long rural or peripheral
trips. MobiRoute deliberately forbids transfers today (a wheelchair user should
not be asked to change vehicles); this is the citation that shows the choice is
informed rather than accidental.

---

## 3. Interrelated and return trips

Johnsen, L. C., Meisel, F., Ehmke, J. F. — *Stochastic stay times for
interrelated trips in the rural dial-a-ride problem*. **Transportation Research
Part E 195 (2025)**.

Outbound and return legs of the same person are coupled through a stay time
that is uncertain (a medical appointment overruns).

**Relevance.** Highest-priority external result for us. Our linked-trip contract
(`docs/linked-trip-contract.md`) currently allows a plan that serves the
outbound leg and rejects the return; this paper is both the argument for why
that is unacceptable in service terms and a template for modelling it as a
stochastic stay time instead of a hard window.

## 4. Learning-based and hybrid heuristics

- Jacobsen, T., Lischka, A., Kulcsár, B., Lindman, M. (Chalmers / Volvo Cars) — *Learning to Dial-a-Ride: A Deep Graph Reinforcement Learning Approach to the Electric Dial-a-Ride Problem*, arXiv:2601.22052 (2026). Graph-based policy learning; positioned against insertion and LNS baselines.
- *A hybrid large neighborhood search algorithm for the integrated dial-a-ride problem using electric vehicles*, arXiv:2512.03562 (2025).
- Ropke, S., Pisinger, D. (2006) adaptive LNS remains the baseline every 2025–2026 heuristic reports against.

**Relevance.** Our ALNS solver is in the right family, but we have never
compared it against a published baseline under a comparable cost algebra.
Until we do, the correct phrasing is "ALNS-family heuristic implemented", not
"competitive with the state of the art".

---

## 5. Approximation results

Zhao, J., Xiao, M. — *Improved Approximations for Dial-a-Ride Problems*.
arXiv:2601.21652 (2026).

**Relevance.** Background only. Useful when an expert asks whether the problem
is "just TSP": it is not, and the approximation literature quantifies the gap.

## 6. Deployed paratransit systems

Pavia et al. — IJCAI 2024, pp. 7430–7437, DOI `10.24963/ijcai.2024/822`, with
the SmartTransit.AI demonstration DOI `10.24963/ijcai.2024/1028`. Deployment
with CARTA (Chattanooga), roughly 200 paratransit requests per day in a metro
of about 1.8 M people (arXiv:2203.15127).

**Relevance.** The closest published analogue of the MobiRoute pilot: same order
of magnitude of daily requests, same public-operator setting, and a documented
evaluation protocol on historical trips before any live deployment. Our pilot
protocol (32 paired days) is deliberately modelled on this pattern.

## 7. What MobiRoute claims from this review

**May claim:**

- Our constraint set (wheelchair capacity, time windows, ride-time limits, linked trips, operator policy) is the constraint set the 2025–2026 literature works with.
- Our modelling choices (no transfers, minute discretization, insertion + ALNS) are documented decisions with known trade-offs and known references.
- The gap between our kernel and published exact results is unmeasured **by design of the claim gate**, not by omission.

**May not claim:**

- Any comparison, ranking or gap against the works above.
- That an ALNS or RL implementation existing in the repository implies state-of-the-art quality.
- That deployment evidence from CARTA transfers to Moscow conditions.

## 8. Roadmap implications, ranked

1. **Return-chain integrity** (Johnsen et al. 2025): make "outbound served, return rejected" impossible or explicitly flagged as an incident. Highest service impact, lowest implementation cost.
2. **Profile A loader** (Gaul et al. 2025): reproduce the literature cost algebra so a gap becomes publishable at all.
3. **Synchronized visits** (Zhao et al. 2026): model escort/attendant requirements.
4. **Bench protocol against ALNS baseline** (Ropke & Pisinger 2006): comparable-algebra runs before any quality claim.
5. **Transfers** (ATMOS 2025): only if a rural or inter-district use case appears.
